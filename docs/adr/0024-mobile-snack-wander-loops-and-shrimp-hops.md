# ADR 0024: Mobile snack wander loops and shrimp hops

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-24
- Amends: [ADR 0021](0021-danger-pass-chase-rule-and-moving-hazards.md) (replaces only its tadpole lane and shrimp spot (its ND-22 to ND-24, and the lane and spot names in its phase 1 contract), its keep-out check (its ND-26), its spit rule (its ND-26), its snack ramp tadpole speeds (its ND-31) and its magnet-pulled lane residual, and adds shrimp hops beside its shrimp flick (its ND-25); the ADR 0021 rules this ADR does not replace stay in force as amended by [ADR 0022](0022-danger-pass-phase-2-seam-amendments.md) and [ADR 0023](0023-whole-water-danger-test-and-relaxed-fairness.md) (#130))
- Related: [ADR 0007](0007-kid-safety-and-privacy.md) (snacks stay gentle: never hurt, scared or eaten on screen), [ADR 0013](0013-axolotl-rig-and-frame-budget.md) (frame time with more moving snacks), [ADR 0022](0022-danger-pass-phase-2-seam-amendments.md) (living docs name no area; an area re-records its own golden when its snack data changes)

This ADR records the build choices for issue #131, the shared snack movement rules: the owner's decisions 1 to 7 on #131 (2026-10-24), which are final, and the named defaults ND-1 to ND-18 (PRD open questions 354 to 371; ND-k is open question 353 + k), which are provisional. The owner can override any named default on the implementing PR. ND numbers here are issue #131's; ADR 0021's are written "ADR 0021's ND-n".

## Context

ADR 0021 gave each tadpole a short straight lane, 80 to 160 px long, which it swam there and back, and each freshwater shrimp one spot, where it rested until the head came within its trigger radius and then flicked away. One screen is 480 px wide, so a lane was a third of a screen at most, and a shrimp the player never came near never moved. The owner's play test (2026-10-24) found that the snacks "barely even move", and the owner decided:

1. Tadpoles never stop. Each one wanders its own home area, about one screen wide, from near the surface to near the bed, and changes direction every few seconds.
2. Tadpole speeds rise by area, from 40 to 70 px/s, all under a one-leg axolotl's 90 px/s.
3. Shrimp are always busy. Each one hops around its own small home area every 1 to 2 s, and still flicks away when the head comes close.
4. Snack counts do not change.
5. PRD H4 records the movement rules, so every later area gets them.
6. The keep-out rule is relaxed for tadpoles and shrimp alike. A predator may reach part of a home, but each snack keeps coming back to safe water.
7. The short in-between state is fine: until each area's retune places real homes, its tadpoles swim their old lanes faster and its shrimp only flick.

ADR 0021's keep-out check kept a whole lane farther than every B10 threat's strike range. A home a screen wide that reaches nearly the full depth cannot meet that beside a full danger bar's predators, so decision 6 needs a new check.

Constraints:

- No `rand()` draw and no seed. ADR 0021's "fixed area data, not seeded" holds: `buildZone` still appends mobile snacks after the seeded worms and bugs with no draw, so seeded snacks, placements and the encounter and trip traces stay the same.
- Ids, kinds, fact cards and the save do not change: `'snack-tadpole-<n>'` and `'snack-shrimp-<n>'`, no new save field and no `SAVE_VERSION` bump.
- `src/logic/` stays pure, and the scene stays thin ([ADR 0006](0006-testing-strategy.md)). No new textures and no new dependencies.
- The tadpole speed cap stays `TADPOLES.maxSpeed` 72 px/s, so every Snack magnet pull stays at or under 86.4 px/s.
- Worms and bugs do not change.

## Decision

**Wander loops (ND-1, ND-2, ND-3).** A tadpole swims a closed wander loop of turn points written as numbers in its area's layout file, built with `tadpoleWander(rule, points, offset)` in `src/logic/mobileSnacks.ts`, and swims it at its area's steady speed, forever, turning at each point. Its place comes from the snack drift clock alone, `along = ((t + offset) x speed) mod lap`, from leg lengths worked out once at build time, so there is no per-trip tadpole state. `tadpoleWander` takes 2 to 8 points, the first its start, and throws a `RangeError` when the speed is outside its limit, a value is not finite, a leg takes less than 1.5 s or more than 5 s, the lap takes more than 40 s, or the offset is outside [0, lap). A loop counts as wandering, by `meetsSnackMotion(layout)`, when it has 4 to 8 points and its home area, the box around the loop, is 320 to 480 px wide and 150 to 360 px tall (taller areas aim for about 200 px), with every point inside the swim bounds and below the highest wave. The `offset` puts an area's tadpoles out of step. The limits are `TADPOLES` in `src/logic/tuning.ts`, which loses `laneMin` and `laneMax`; `maxSpeed` stays 72.

**Smooth turns, drawing only (ND-4, ND-10, ND-11).** The tadpole's place stays exactly on its loop, so every proof uses straight legs. The sprite faces along the leg's sideways part and flips at the corner; a near-vertical leg (its sideways part under a quarter of its length) keeps the facing it had. Its tilt is the leg's angle from level, held to 40 degrees either way, eased (smoothstep) from the leg before's over 0.25 s after each corner, and adds to the tail sway. Under reduced motion it still swims its loop, because that is gameplay, with tail frame 0, no sway and its tilt changing at once. `mobileSnackPose` writes the pose into the view's own object, so `MobileSnackView` allocates nothing per frame.

**Shrimp hops (ND-5, ND-6, ND-10, ND-11).** A shrimp has a home of 1 to 6 hop spots, built with `shrimpHome(points)`, the first its start. A home counts as hopping, by `meetsSnackMotion`, when it has 3 to 6 spots in a box at most 200 x 160 px. When its rest ends and the head is farther than 120 px, a shrimp with 2 or more spots hops toward its next spot: at most 2/3 of its flick distance, at 2/3 of its flick speed, landing on the spot when it is that close and then aiming at the one after. It never hops while the head is within 120 px. It rests 3/4 of its rest time after a hop and its full rest time after a flick, and shrimp n of N first rests (n - 1) / N of a hop rest. A hop lands only where the hard rules below hold, with each plastic worm where it is now, and only when its whole glide there is clear (below); when the next spot is not clear it tries the one after, and when none is, it rests again. A hop is always slower than its flick, so the shrimp's top speed for the Snack magnet does not change. The flick still wins: its trigger, distance, speed and catch limit are as before, and a hopping shrimp's flick lands only inside its home box grown by the flick distance, after which it hops back. A home of one spot never hops and flicks by ADR 0021's rules. The shrimp faces the head while the head is within 120 px, while it flicks and while it rests after a flick, and otherwise the way it last hopped; it shows its flick frame only while flicking. Under reduced motion its hops and flicks land at once. The new `SHRIMP` limits sit beside the old ones in `tuning.ts`.

**Where mobile snacks may go (ND-7, owner decision 6; replaces ADR 0021's keep-out check).**

- Hard rules, for every tadpole turn point and leg box and every shrimp hop spot, hop leg and landing (with its drift and bob box): inside the swim bounds and below the highest wave; out of the Home Spot ring, the safe area, every current and every mat pair's whole sweep; and 80 px or more from every plastic worm and every hazard shape, a drifting ghost net by its whole drift path. `mobileSnackHardClear(box, avoid)` checks them.
- Whole glides: a shrimp's flick or hop starts only when, at every point on its way, its drift and bob box meets the hard rules, except the plastic worms where they are now. Clear ends alone do not make a clear way: a straight glide between two clear landings can still cross a current, a mat sweep, the safe area, the Home Spot ring or a hazard's 80 px. `shrimpGlideClear(from, to, drift, avoid)` checks it exactly, measuring each rule from the glide's path to the rule's shape grown by the shrimpBox's half size, and allocates nothing. `shrimpHomeClear` checks it on every hop leg, and `stepShrimpFlicks` on every flick and hop, skipped spots and flick landings included. A plastic worm counts only at the landing: a spat worm moves, and a shrimp resting beside one must still be able to glide away from it.
- Threat rule, tadpoles: a predator or moving hazard may reach part of the loop, but the longest stretch of the loop out of every threat's reach is at least half the loop (`TADPOLES.safeLoopShare`), sampled at most 2 px apart. So every tadpole spends at least half of each lap, in one stretch, where it can be caught without danger. `tadpoleWanderClear(wander, avoid)` checks both.
- Threat rule, shrimp: at least half of a shrimp's hop spots (`SHRIMP.safeSpotShare`), each by its drift and bob box, are out of every threat's reach. `shrimpHomeClear(home, drift, avoid)` checks both.
- Reach is B10's S from `zoneThreats`: a predator's lunge range from its patrol loop, with no leash, and a moving hazard's touch reach from its sweep. A threat rule exists at all because the exit needs every snack eaten (H2), so a snack that lived inside a threat's reach would force a risk.
- `mobileSnackClear` stays, the strict check: a one-spot shrimp's flick landing still needs it, and a converted area's layout test still proves it.

**The spit rule (ND-7; replaces ADR 0021's spit rule).** A spat plastic worm never lands within 80 px of a tadpole leg's box or a shrimp hop leg's box. `mobileLegBoxes(zone)` gives one box per leg, tadpoles first, for `SpitAvoid.keepClear`; a home of one spot has no legs. SwimScene passes `keepClear` only when there is at least one leg box. `src/logic/plasticWorms.ts` does not change. A slanted leg's box is bigger than the leg, so a few more spits stay where they were.

**Catch proof (ND-8).** `oneLegCatchesTadpole(rule, wander, bounds)` steers a one-leg axolotl (`legSwimTuning(1)`, on the game's swim step) straight at where the tadpole is drawn, from 8 starts 100 px outside its home box, and asks that its head reach centre comes within the 36 px eat reach within 20 s from each. Each area's retune runs it on every tadpole in that area. The shrimp catch limit stays (rest x 70 >= trigger radius + flick distance - 36), and no hop starts within 120 px of the head.

**Speeds, the ramp and the magnet (ND-9; replaces ADR 0021's ramp tadpole speeds).** The tadpole speed rows of `SNACK_RAMP` (PRD H4), in ramp order after the row with no mobile snacks, become 35 to 45, 45 to 55, 55 to 65 and 65 to 72 px/s, and the rule for later areas 70 to 72 px/s ("at least the area before's"). Mobile shares, snack counts and shrimp values stay. The Snack magnet formula stays (max(40, 1.2 x the fastest mobile snack), ADR 0021's ND-28), so at the rows' middle speeds of 40, 50, 60 and 70 px/s the pulls are 48, 60, 72 and 84 px/s, all under 86.4. A tadpole faster than 72 px/s needs a new owner decision on the cap; later areas can get harder with more tadpoles and longer loops instead. Each area's own speed and pull are in its N2 paragraph and Tuning values rows.

**Data shape (ND-13).** `MobileSnackMix` is `{ tadpole?, wanders, shrimp?, homes }`. `Collectible.wander` (a `SnackWander`: the turn points from the stored start, the leg ends, the lap, the speed and the offset) replaces `lane`, and a shrimp spawn carries `home`, its hop spots. `TadpoleLane`, `ShrimpSpot`, `SnackLane`, `tadpoleLane`, `tadpoleLaneBox`, `tadpoleLaneBoxes` and `tadpoleLaneCycle` are gone, and no old lane code is left.

**Plain conversion of areas with mobile snacks (ND-14, owner decision 7).** Each shipped lane becomes a 2-point loop from its start to its end, the same place swum there and back; each shipped shrimp spot becomes a one-spot home that flicks and does not hop; each tadpole speed moves to its ramp row's middle; and each offset keeps its share of the new lap. Those areas' layout tests follow the new names, speeds and pulls, and prove both the strict check and the new clear checks on the converted data, but do not assert `meetsSnackMotion`. Each area's retune issue (#132 to #135) places real loops and homes and adds `meetsSnackMotion` and the catch proof to that area's layout test. Each changed area re-records its own golden once, on purpose (ADR 0022); an area without mobile snacks keeps its golden byte-identical.

**The dev area shows the rules (ND-14).** `DEV_DANGER_AREA` in `src/logic/devAreas.ts` gets real data with the top ramp row's values: three tadpoles at 70 px/s on 5-point wander loops, a third of a lap out of step, and three shrimp on 4-spot homes, with the shrimp rule `shrimpRule(60, 1.2, 60, 145)` (so a 0.9 s rest after a hop, and 40 px hops at about 97 px/s), and an 84 px/s Snack magnet pull. Its counts, size, safe area, threats and bounds do not change. `devAreas.test.ts` proves `meetsSnackMotion`, `tadpoleWanderClear`, `shrimpHomeClear` and the catch proof on every tadpole.

**Performance (ND-12).** A tadpole's place is a short walk over at most 8 legs with nothing allocated per frame. A shrimp's clear check runs only when a hop or a flick would start, about once a second per shrimp, except for a ready shrimp within its trigger radius whose flick landing is blocked: it runs its landing clear check again every frame, allocating nothing (issue #139), until the landing clears or the head moves away (see the blocked-flick follow-up below). The avoid data is still rebuilt only when a plastic worm moves.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| A smooth closed curve (Lissajous) for each tadpole | Its speed is not steady (it slows at the edges), it cannot bend round a keep-out, and it looks mechanical. |
| Turn points from a fixed hash of the area id and snack number | No `rand()` draw either, but nobody can see or hand-place the loop, and changing the hash moves every area. |
| Seeded `rand()` draws for loops and homes | ADR 0021 already rejected these: new draws move every later draw, and every seed's loops would need proof. |
| A home rectangle stored as its own data, with the loop inside it | Two things to keep in step, for no gain. The home area is the box around the loop. |
| A fixed 2 s per leg | Too regular to look like wandering. |
| Rounded corners in the path itself | Makes the keep-out proofs and the catch proof harder. The turn is eased in the drawing only. |
| A separate hop distance, speed and rest per area | Three more values to tune per area, and "every 1 to 2 s" already follows from each area's rest. |
| No hops under reduced motion | Makes the game easier with reduced motion on, and the shrimp would end up somewhere other than under full motion. |
| Keep ADR 0021's strict keep-out check | Homes must shrink or bend far round the predators, so a home a screen wide at all depths does not fit beside a full danger bar. |
| Ignore threats for mobile snacks | The exit needs every snack eaten, so a snack inside a threat's reach would force a risk. |
| Faster tail frames for faster tadpoles | Nothing looks wrong at 8 frames a second. |

## Consequences

- Tadpoles swim all the time, all round their homes, and shrimp keep hopping between rests. Worms and bugs do not change.
- Until each area's retune, an area with mobile snacks plays with faster tadpoles on their old lanes and shrimp that only flick (owner decision 7).
- Every area with mobile snacks proves `meetsSnackMotion`, the clear checks and the catch proof in its own layout test once its retune places real homes. PRD H4 holds the rules, so later areas get them too.
- A spat plastic worm keeps clear of every tadpole leg box and shrimp hop leg box.
- Ids, kinds, fact cards and the save are unchanged, so no `SAVE_VERSION` bump is needed.
- [ADR 0021](0021-danger-pass-chase-rule-and-moving-hazards.md) status points here for the amended parts.

Recorded residual:

- The Snack magnet moves a tadpole's whole loop (kept from ADR 0021, where it was the whole lane). Root cause: `pullSnacksToward` in `src/logic/collectibles.ts` moves the snack's stored x and y, and `drawnSnackX` and `drawnSnackY` draw the wander loop from them, so the whole loop moves with the pull and can briefly cross a keep-out; a loop the player pulls and then leaves stays where it was left for the rest of the trip. Bounded: the pull, max(40, 1.2 x the fastest mobile snack), is always faster than the tadpole, so a pulled tadpole closes on the head and is eaten soon. Not changed: the obvious fix, a separate pull offset that decays back to the loop, adds per-trip tadpole state, against ND-1's place from the drift clock alone and against the catch proofs, which assume that place.
- A queasy one-leg axolotl (54 px/s) or a snagged one (45 px/s) cannot catch a 70 px/s tadpole until it recovers, after 3 s or 2 s. Bounded: ADR 0021's 60 px/s tadpoles had the same kind of gap, and a Tail Dash (330 px/s) always catches one. Not changed: the owner set the speeds (decision 2).
- The Snack magnet pull stays below a shrimp's own hop and flick speeds (owner decision on issue #131, 2026-10-24, before the PR). It answers the finding "Magnet speed is below a shrimp's true movement speed" (local Codex review, finding 1d29f724, given for tracing only; this recorded decision is the key). Root cause: `snackTopSpeed` in `src/logic/mobileSnacks.ts` measures a shrimp as flickDistance / (restFor + flickDistance / flickSpeed) (ADR 0021's ND-28), the farthest it gets in a second over one rest and one flick, not its speed during a flick or a hop; hops are not counted, because a hop is shorter and slower than a flick (at most 2/3 of the flick distance at 2/3 of the flick speed, `SHRIMP.hopShare`). Bounded: the pull is max(40, 1.2 x the fastest top speed), so by construction it is at least 1.2 x a shrimp's top speed; the magnet pulls only inside its radius (perk cap 70 px), where a shrimp never hops, because the head is inside `SHRIMP.holdDistance` (120 px); and the shrimp catch limit (rest x 70 >= trigger radius + flick distance - 36) still lets a one-leg axolotl catch it without the magnet. Not changed: the owner decided to keep it, so a later finding contesting this is a repeat.
- Frame time is not measured before the PR; it stays an owner check in the PR (owner decision on issue #131, 2026-10-24). It answers the finding "The mandatory frame-time acceptance gate has not been run" (local Codex review, finding f78f389a, given for tracing only; this recorded decision is the key). Ground: the [ADR 0013](0013-axolotl-rig-and-frame-budget.md) budget is measured with `?perf` on the owner's phone and under DevTools CPU throttling, which no build agent can run. The owner checks Area 5's 19 mobile snacks. Not changed: the owner decided this, so a later finding contesting this is a repeat.

Follow-ups:

- The area retunes #132 to #135 place real wander loops and shrimp homes and prove `meetsSnackMotion`, the clear checks and the catch proof in each area's layout test.
- The owner confirms the provisional named defaults (PRD open questions 354 to 371) on the PR, and checks frame time with the most mobile snacks on their devices (ADR 0013).
- Blocked shrimp flicks allocate on every idle frame: deferred to a tracked GitHub issue, linked from the issue #131 pull request (owner decision on issue #131, 2026-10-24). It answers the finding "Blocked shrimp flicks allocate on every idle frame" (local Codex review, finding 683070d8, given for tracing only; this recorded decision is the key). Root cause: `startFrom` in `src/logic/shrimpFlick.ts` leaves a ready shrimp within its trigger radius whose flick landing is not clear at the same `readyAt`, so every frame `flickFrom` builds a landing point and a shrimpBox and runs the landing clear check again, while `stepShrimpFlicks` returns null. Bounded: only a ready shrimp within its trigger radius of the head, in practice one at a time; two small objects and one clear check a frame; no items array and no new state. Resolved by issue #139: the landing check, now `isShrimpFlickLandingClear`, writes into reused scratch objects, so a blocked flick still runs its clear check each frame but allocates nothing.
