# ADR 0021: Danger pass shared parts: the chase rule, moving hazards and mobile snacks

- Status: Accepted. Amended in part by [ADR 0022](0022-danger-pass-phase-2-seam-amendments.md) (#121), [ADR 0023](0023-whole-water-danger-test-and-relaxed-fairness.md) (#130) and [ADR 0024](0024-mobile-snack-wander-loops-and-shrimp-hops.md) (#131)
- Date: 2026-10-24
- Related: [ADR 0007](0007-kid-safety-and-privacy.md) (the new parts stay gentle, and the fact cards stay true), [ADR 0012](0012-save-on-device.md) (new ids under its additive rule), [ADR 0013](0013-axolotl-rig-and-frame-budget.md) (frame time with moving parts), [ADR 0014](0014-area-framework.md) (the framework this builds on), [ADR 0015](0015-multi-area-catalogue-and-slow-only-hazards.md) (the slow-only hazard model the drifting nets keep), [ADR 0016](0016-heron-timer-warnings-and-area-look-variants.md) (the snack dart rule), [ADR 0018](0018-chinampa-gardens-ghost-nets-and-predator-ladder.md) (ghost nets, per-area tilapia tuning and the predator ladder), [ADR 0019](0019-heron-wading-stalker.md) (shared species rules), [ADR 0020](0020-u-turn-areas-garter-snake-and-hyacinth-mats.md) (`warnOnRoute`, element fact cards and the mats)

This ADR records the build choices for issue #117, phase 1 of the danger pass: the named defaults ND-1 to ND-31 (PRD open questions 248 to 278; ND-n is open question 247 + n) and the owner's decisions on #117 (2026-10-23). The owner can override any named default on the implementing PR.

## Context

The danger pass (epic #32, [PRD](../prds/axolotl-mvp.md) N2) makes later areas busier: predators that chase, hazards that move, and snacks that swim. The owner split it in two (#117, 2026-10-23). Phase 1, this issue, builds the shared parts only, and every part is opt-in and off in every real area. Phase 2 puts them into the real areas, one strain per area: #121 (Area 2), #120 (Area 3), #119 (Area 4) and #118 (Area 5).

Six parts did not fit the framework as it was:

- A predator only patrolled its route and warned when the axolotl came near. Nothing followed the axolotl.
- Every hazard that costs a leg had a fixed shape. Timing elements ran on a clock, but never cost a leg.
- Ghost nets never moved.
- Every snack bobbed in place. The one-time darter (ADR 0016) was the only snack that moved.
- The one-new-thing rule (ADR 0014) would count every danger part as a new threat, so an area could not add the carp and a punting pole beside its own new predator.
- Only the tilapia had per-area tuning (ADR 0018).

Constraints:

- Ids are saved or keyed on (ADR 0012), so every new id is stable forever.
- Areas 1 to 5 play exactly as before. An Area 5 golden (`src/logic/chinampa2Golden.test.ts`) was recorded from main in its own first commit, before any shared change (ND-16). In phase 1 (#117) all five goldens stay byte-identical; each phase 2 strain re-records only its own (amended by ADR 0022).
- `SNACK_DART`, `src/logic/snackDart.ts`, `src/logic/perks.ts` and their tests do not change.
- Game rules stay pure, in `src/logic/` ([ADR 0006](0006-testing-strategy.md)).
- No network requests, and fact cards stay true ([ADR 0007](0007-kid-safety-and-privacy.md)).
- No new save field and no `SAVE_VERSION` bump.
- Phase 2 strains mostly add data to their own area's files. No phase 2 strain may have to append to a shared list (ND-14).

Provisional choices are PRD open questions; each names its row.

## Decision

**The chase rule, opt-in tuning (ND-2, ND-3, ND-4).** A predator chases only when its tuning has `chase`. `TILAPIA`, `HERON`, `GARTER_SNAKE` and `CARP` have none, so without a chase `stepPredator` plays step for step as before.

- The chase happens only on patrol. When the predator may warn (A1, A3), it warns as before. Otherwise, when the axolotl is exposed (outside the safe area, and for a sight hunter not in cover) and within `sight` px, and the predator is within its leash of its patrol loop, it swims toward the axolotl at `speed`.
- It stops just inside its lungeRange (1e-6 px inside), where it warns, or waits in place while another predator is warning or striking (A1).
- No chase step ends more than `leash` px from the patrol loop (`routeLoopDistance`). A step that would go farther ends on the leash's edge, on the line from the nearest point of the loop. The bounds and the safe-area keep-out (`moveClamped`) apply as for any move. When these clamps conflict, the predator holds its place. It faces the way it moves.
- Beyond its leash (after a strike, say), or with the axolotl out of sight or in the safe area, it swims its route at patrol speed, which brings it back. The warning, the strike and the cooldown do not change.
- Chasing is not attacking, so any number of predators may chase at once. There is no new cue, and reduced motion does not change the chase.
- The warn-on-route rule (ADR 0020). A species with `warnOnRoute` chases along its route only: its leash counts as 0, whatever its data says. So the garter snake slides along its stretch, and the #103 depth limits hold.
- Limits, checked by `tunedSpecies` with a RangeError: the speed is strictly between the 2-leg and 4-leg top speeds (112.5 and 150 px/s, worked out from `SWIM.maxSpeed` and `LEGS.speedByLegs`); the sight is finite and greater than the lungeRange of the tuning that carries it; the leash is finite and 0 or more. It also refuses a chase on a species with the `fixedStrikeSpot` or `stillCooldown` rule (the heron), and NaN or Infinity anywhere. A snag, queasy time or lost legs can leave the axolotl slower than a chaser; Tail Dash (330 px/s) always outruns one.
- Defaults for phase 2: `TILAPIA_CHASE` (full bar) sight 240, leash 160, speed 125; `TILAPIA_RAMP_CHASE` (ramp bar) 200, 80, 115; `GARTER_SNAKE_CHASE` 200, 0, 120; `CARP_CHASE` 220, 120, 115. The heron has none; it keeps its wading stalk.
- `tunedSpecies(species, tuning)` gives a species with the same id, name, meet range, fact card, hook and rules, played with its own tuning. So any species can take per-area tuning in its area's own layout file, and the views, fact cards, meeting and the Collection Book still treat it as that species.

**The risk ladder ignores the chase (ND-5).** A chasing predator's strike reach for hiding spots (B10) stays its lungeRange, measured from its patrol loop, and the gap measure keeps it out by the same reach. With the leash added (S = lungeRange + leash), a Common spot in a full-bar area would need to be about 365 px from every tilapia loop, which leaves almost no Common spots. The chase widens where a predator can warn by the same leash for every spot near it, so the ladder's order still holds. Only `dangerBar.ts` and the fairness harness count the leash. The other option is S = lungeRange + leash (Alternatives).

**The common carp on the shared rules (ND-6, ND-7).** The carp is a species with its own tuning and no rule options: `CARP` is minWindup 0.8, windup 1.0, lungeRange 120, lungeSpeed 200, lungeDuration 0.6 (a 120 px sweep), hitRadius 38, contactRadius 41, bodyRadius 32, cooldown 2.5, patrolSpeed 35, bumpSpeed 160 and bumpCooldown 0.6. It is met within 180 px, a touch is a gentle bump, and its B10 S is 120. Its warning is a nose-down tip with a small mud puff (inked in greyscale, still under reduced motion), and its strike is a slow, wide tail sweep along the aim locked at the end of the warning. It is never shown biting or holding the axolotl (ADR 0007). Its fact card, "Meet the carp", cites its sources in `factCards.ts` and PRD C13. No new rule path was needed: the shared rules (ADR 0014, ADR 0019) already cover it.

**The moving hazard type (ND-8, ND-9, ND-10).** A moving hazard is a straight shaft that comes down from the surface (y 80) at its x on a fixed cycle: rest, warn (the telegraph), jab, hold and lift. Its type is data: id, name, look id, the five phase times, depth, shaft half width, touch reach, meet range and fact card. `movingHazardSpawn()` throws a RangeError when the warning is under the 0.8 s floor (`TILAPIA.minWindup`), a time is negative, the period is 0 or the depth is not above 0.

- A touch is the body centre within the touch reach of the shaft. It costs one leg through the shared hit rules (A4, A5, A11, A22): protected time, the Shell block and `legsLost`, so it counts for Star 3. It is never a bump, and the hit comes from the tip. At rest and during the warning it touches nothing.
- Each encounter step checks the predators, then still hazards, then moving hazards, then the bump, and at most one hit lands per step. The touch is checked at the advanced `hazardTime`, the same time the view draws.
- The clock. `EncounterState` has `movingHazards` and its own `hazardTime` only when the area has moving hazards, so every encounter state today stays the same. `hazardTime` starts at 0 each trip (`createEncounter` and `newTrip`) and moves on only in `stepEncounter`, by the same capped step, so it stops with the pause, the banking card and the carry home.
- Why not an element: elements never cost a leg. Element types are timing and puzzle data, and the hit rules belong to hazards (ADR 0014). A pole whose touch costs a leg is a hazard.
- Why not a still hazard: a still hazard has a fixed shape and no clock; `createEncounter` copies it as {id, type, shape}. The pole's shape changes with time, so it needs a clock, and its hit must land in the encounter step that owns the hit rules. Keeping that clock in the encounter keeps the hit and the clock in one place, and leaves every still hazard as it was.
- Placement and risk: its B10 shape is its full sweep, the jab rect at full depth, with S its touch reach. It is met within its meet range of its sweep, outside the safe area. The gap measure treats its sweep as a shape kept out by its touch reach, and snack starts keep 80 px from it. Phase 2 keeps each sweep farther than every predator's lungeRange from that predator's loop.
- Looks are data. `MovingHazardView` draws the textures baked for `type.look` and throws when a look has none. `MOVING_HAZARD_LOOKS` maps a look id to its painters, so a new look is one painter file and one entry. BootScene bakes a look only when some area has a moving hazard with it. The view reuses its sprites each frame. It draws the rest-position tip 24 px above the surface and dips it in the warning (drawing only). Under reduced motion the shadow and the ring show still, and the jab still moves, because it is gameplay.
- The punting pole: `PUNTING_POLES` is a 4 s cycle (rest 1.6 s, warn 1.0 s, jab 0.3 s, hold 0.5 s, lift 0.6 s), depth 180 px (the tip reaches y 260), half width 5 px, touch reach 18 px and meet range 180 px. Its look is a trajinera's bright bow on the surface with no people; its warning darkens the boat's shadow and dips the tip to the surface with a ripple ring. Its fact card is "Trajineras".

**Drifting ghost nets on the element clock (ND-11).** A ghost net with `drift` moves back and forth along the straight path from its place to its place plus (dx, dy), slowing at both ends: its share of the path is (1 − cos(2π(t + offset) / period)) / 2, where t is the trip's `elementTime`. A net with no drift never moves, and `hazardShapeAt` returns its own shape. `DRIFTING_NETS` sets a 16 s period and a path of at most 200 px, and `netDrift()` refuses a longer path or a period of 0. A drifting net stays slow-only: it snags where it is now (N3), never costs a leg, and is not a B10 threat, so it needs no hit and no encounter clock. The ghost nets already live in trip state, so they use the trip's element clock; the snag and the meeting use the element time the step reaches. It is met within 100 px of where it is now. Every placement rule uses the whole drift path (`hazardSweep`, the shape's bounding rect over the path): snack starts, mobile snack lanes and spots, the gap measure, and phase 2's net rules. The mats' push-out checks each net where it is now. Under reduced motion it still drifts, with no extra sway.

**The danger bar and the ramp bar (ND-12).** `src/logic/dangerBar.ts` measures the bars the PRD sets (N2): `movingThreatCount`, `predatorTypeCount`, `movingHazardTypeCount`, `longestQuietStretch`, `meetsFullDangerBar` and `meetsRampDangerBar`. An area's screens are (width / 480) × (height / 360). "In reach" is within lungeRange plus leash of a predator's loop, or within the touch reach of a moving hazard's sweep, sampled every 1 px along the main path. Its tests use made-up layouts and the dev area, and assert nothing about Areas 1 to 5; each phase 2 strain proves the bar in its own layout test.

**Danger parts outside the one-new-thing rule (ND-13).** A species with `dangerPart: true` (the carp) and every moving hazard type are danger parts. `newThingsByArea` leaves them out, so `areas.test.ts`'s per-area pins and its one-new-thing limit stay as they are when phase 2 adds them. The mark lives on the species or comes with the type; it is never a shared list of ids, so no strain appends to one. `threatIds`, the fact cards and meeting still include the danger parts. Mobile snack types are snacks, not threats or elements, so neither the one-new-thing rule nor the danger bar counts them (ND-27).

**Mobile snacks (ND-22 to ND-31).** The tadpole swims there and back along a short straight lane at its area's speed. The freshwater shrimp rests, then flicks backward away from the head when it comes near, again after each rest. Both count as one snack each, like a worm or a bug, for the counter, the last-3 sparkle, the exit (H2, H3), the snack speed-up (A19) and the reset when carried home (A12). Worms and bugs keep bobbing.

- Fixed area data, not seeded (ND-23). Tadpole lanes and shrimp spots are written out as numbers in the area's own layout file, built with `mobileSnackMix()`. `buildZone` appends them after the seeded worms and bugs, tadpoles first, then shrimp, in data order, with no `rand()` draw. Why: mobile snacks add no random draw, so a strain that does not lower `snackWorms` or `snackBugs` keeps its seeded snacks' draws, and a strain that lowers them re-draws its area's seeded snacks and re-records only its own golden on purpose (amended by ADR 0022); each lane and spot is proven once in the area's layout test, not for every seed; and a lane must sit clear of threats along its whole length, which a seeded stripe cannot promise. An area without `mobileSnacks` builds exactly as before, and its `ZoneLayout` has no `mobileSnacks` key. Phase 2 lowers `snackWorms` and `snackBugs` by the mobile count, so each area keeps its total, and every snack that starts in a current is a worm or a bug; `snacksInCurrent` is a floor (amended by ADR 0022).
- The drawn place is the one source (ND-24). `drawnSnackX` and `drawnSnackY` give where a snack is drawn. For a tadpole that is a point on its lane from `tadpoleLaneCycle` on the snack drift clock, clamped to the swim bounds, with no drift and no bob, the same with and without reduced motion. For worms, bugs and resting shrimp they give the same numbers as before. Eating (`collectSnacksAtHead`), the Snack magnet (`pullSnacksToward`), the dart triggers (the one-time darter and the shrimp flick), meeting (`metSnackTypeIds`) and rendering (SwimScene and `MobileSnackView`) all read it, so a snack is always eaten, pulled, darted and met where the player sees it. The stored x and y are the lane start, and the magnet moves them as for any snack. The wiggle (4 tail frames at 8 a second and a ±8° sway) is drawing only.
- The shrimp is a separate repeat rule beside the untouched darter (ND-25, ND-27). `stepShrimpFlicks` flicks a ready shrimp straight away from the head by `flickDistance` when it is within `triggerRadius` (inclusive), at `flickSpeed` (at once under reduced motion), only when the box its drift and bob can reach at the target passes `mobileSnackClear`. After landing it rests `restFor` seconds on the drift clock, with no limit on flicks, and it returns null, allocating nothing, when nothing moves. `shrimpRule()` refuses values outside `SHRIMP`, including the catch limit restFor × 70 ≥ triggerRadius + flickDistance − 36, so a one-leg axolotl swimming straight at a just-landed shrimp reaches it within one rest. `SNACK_DART` and its one-time darters do not change. An area whose layout has shrimp gets no one-time darters: `oneTimeDarts(area, zone)` returns null. Otherwise it picks the darters from the worms and bugs only, so Areas 1 to 5 get exactly today's darter ids. Phase 2 leaves `snackDart: SNACK_DART` in its AREAS entry, and the shrimp turns it off.
- The per-area magnet pull (ND-28). Each trip's pull is `snackMagnetSpeed(base, zone)` = max(base, 1.2 × the fastest top speed of the area's mobile snacks), where base is the perk's 40 px/s. A tadpole's top speed is its speed; a shrimp's is flickDistance / (restFor + flickDistance / flickSpeed). SwimScene works it out once per trip. A pulled snack always closes on a still head, and the 72 px/s tadpole limit keeps every pull at or under 86.4 px/s, under a one-leg axolotl's 90. Why the perk constant is not raised: `SNACK_MAGNET_PULL_SPEED`, `perkTuning` and the perk tests stay as they are, and an area with no mobile snacks gets exactly 40, so Areas 1 to 5 and their goldens are unchanged. One bigger pull for every area would change the perk constant and its tests, and pull harder than needed where the snacks are slow.
- One keep-out check (ND-26). `mobileSnackClear(box, avoid)` is the single check for a tadpole's whole lane and a shrimp's whole drift-and-bob box, at its spot or at a flick target. The box must be inside the swim bounds and below the highest wave; outside the Home Spot ring, the safe area, every current and every mat pair's whole sweep; at least 80 px from every plastic worm where it is now, every hazard shape (a drifting net by its whole drift path), every element sweep and every moving hazard's sweep; and farther than S from every B10 threat (`zoneThreats`). A predator's reach there is its B10 S from its patrol loop, as in ND-5, with the leash not added. Phase 2 proves every lane and spot with it, and at run time each flick target is checked with the plastic worms where they are now. The other option, S + leash, leaves much less room in full-bar areas (Alternatives).
- The spit rule (ND-26). A spat plastic worm never lands within 80 px of a tadpole lane: `SpitAvoid.keepClear` holds the lane boxes (`tadpoleLaneBoxes`), and a landing near one leaves the worm where it was, as a landing in a current does. SwimScene passes `keepClear` only when the area has tadpoles, so nothing changes without lanes.
- Meeting (ND-29). A snack type is met the first time the axolotl's centre is within 120 px of an uncollected snack of that type where it is drawn, outside the safe area. `meetSnacks` adds the id to the trip's `met`, so the card comes due. `sourceFactCards` lists an area's snack type cards after its element cards, tadpole first.
- The snack ramp (ND-31). `SNACK_RAMP` and `meetsSnackRamp(number, layout)` fix the ramp in the PRD (H4): the mobile share and tadpole speed for Areas 1 to 5, and one rule for Area 6 on. A shared test over `AREAS` checks that Area 1 has none and that no ramp value steps down by area number. It holds today, with no area having any, and checks each phase 2 strain as it merges.

**The phase 2 seams (ND-14).** These are phase 2's contract. #121's extra files and the merge order #121, #120, #119, #118 (owner, #124) are in [ADR 0022](0022-danger-pass-phase-2-seam-amendments.md) (amended by ADR 0022).

- A tilapia chase goes in the area's own tilapia tuning in its layout file, as `{ ...<its tuning or TILAPIA>, chase }`. Areas 2 and 3 get a `tilapia` field for the first time. With only `chase` added, their risk bands do not change.
- Any other species chases, or takes per-area tuning, through `tunedSpecies(SPECIES, { ...TUNING, chase })` in the area's layout file, used on its routes. The id stays the same, so the views, fact cards, meeting and the Collection Book treat it as that species.
- Moving hazards: the area's own `movingHazards` list, built with `movingHazardSpawn()`.
- Drifting nets: `drift: netDrift(...)` on the area's own ghost net data.
- Mobile snacks: `mobileSnacks: mobileSnackMix({...})` in the area's own layout file, with its tadpole rule and shrimp rule, its tadpole lanes and its shrimp spots; its `snackWorms` and `snackBugs` lowered by the mobile count, so the total stays the same. Its AreaData in `areas.ts` stays as it is (`snackDrift`, and `snackDart: SNACK_DART` where set), because the shrimp turns the one-time darters off by the shared rule. No strain edits `areas.ts`.
- Proofs live in the area's own files: its layout test, using `dangerBar.ts` and `layoutGaps.ts`, and for snacks its snack counts, `meetsSnackRamp(area number, layout)`, `mobileSnackClear` on every lane and spot with the zone's avoid data, its magnet pull (`snackMagnetSpeed`) and no one-time darters (`oneTimeDarts` gives null); its own fairness file (ND-17); and its own golden, re-recorded on purpose.
- So each strain touches these files, and the snack work adds none:
  - #119 (Area 4): `src/logic/chinampaLayout.ts`, `src/logic/chinampaLayout.test.ts`, `src/logic/chinampaFairness.test.ts` and `src/logic/__snapshots__/chinampaGolden.test.ts.snap`.
  - #118 (Area 5): `src/logic/chinampa2Layout.ts`, `src/logic/chinampa2Layout.test.ts`, `src/logic/chinampa2Fairness.test.ts` and `src/logic/__snapshots__/chinampa2Golden.test.ts.snap`. Its mats rule (no predator reach in a mat sweep) also covers each chaser's leash band.
  - #120 (Area 3): `src/logic/pond2Layout.ts`, `src/logic/pond2Layout.test.ts`, `src/logic/pond2Fairness.test.ts` and `src/logic/__snapshots__/pond2Golden.test.ts.snap`. If it adds a pond look, that is one painter in `src/art`, one entry in `MOVING_HAZARD_LOOKS`, one `MovingHazardType` in `src/logic/movingHazards.ts` and its card in `src/logic/factCards.ts`. ## Alternatives considered

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| The risk ladder counts the leash (S = lungeRange + leash) | The other option in ND-5. A full-bar area would have almost no Common spots, and the ladder's order already holds because the leash widens every spot's reach alike. |
| The moving hazard as a timing element | Elements never cost a leg; the hit rules belong to hazards. |
| The moving hazard as a still hazard with a time-varying shape | A still hazard has no clock. Adding one to every hazard would change every encounter state and the goldens. A separate list with its own clock, present only when set, leaves them as they are. |
| Moving hazards on the trip's element clock | The hits land in the encounter step, so the hazard's clock lives there too, advanced by the same capped step. Drifting nets only snag, which the trip handles, so they use the element clock. |
| A shared list of danger-part ids for the one-new-thing rule | Every phase 2 strain would append to it. The mark on the species or type needs no shared edit. |
| Seeded mobile snacks | New `rand()` draws would move every later draw and change the goldens, and every seed's lanes would need proving clear. |
| Make the existing darter repeat | In phase 1 (#117), `SNACK_DART` and Areas 3 to 5's darters stay byte-identical; an area that adds shrimp turns its one-time darters off (ND-27). A separate shrimp rule leaves them alone (amended by ADR 0022). |
| Each phase 2 strain deletes `snackDart: SNACK_DART` from its AREAS entry | A shared-file edit for every strain. The shrimp turns the darters off by the shared rule instead. |
| One bigger Snack magnet pull for every area | The other option in ND-28. It changes the perk constant and its tests, and the pull in areas with no mobile snacks. |
| Mobile snack keep-out with S + leash | The other option in ND-26. It leaves much less room for lanes and spots in full-bar areas, and B10 already uses S alone (ND-5). |
| A per-area tuning map or per-area snack map in a shared file | Every phase 2 strain would edit it. Each area's own layout file holds its values. |

## Consequences

- Phase 2 strains add danger parts and mobile snacks to their areas as data in their own files, on the seams above. Only #120 may add shared code, and only for a new moving hazard look.
- Any predator species can now chase and take per-area tuning through `tunedSpecies`, beside the tilapia's `tilapia` field (ADR 0018). A species without a chase plays as before.
- An area may have hazards that move on a clock and cost a leg, nets that drift, and snacks that swim. Areas 1 to 5 build, step and draw exactly as before, which the five goldens, the darter tests and the perk tests show. They build and call nothing new per frame.
- The Collection Book gains a card only when some area has it: the carp, pole, tadpole and shrimp cards join the book with the first area that has them (amended by ADR 0022).
- No save change: every new id loads under ADR 0012's additive rule, with no `SAVE_VERSION` bump.
- `src/logic/factCards.ts` is now a runtime leaf module, with no runtime imports. `zone.ts` loads `movingHazards.ts`, which reads `PUNTING_POLE_FACT` while `factCards.ts` would still be loading through its import of `zone.ts`'s `inSafeArea`. `factCards.ts` now mirrors `inSafeArea` privately and imports only types, and `src/logic/moduleCycles.test.ts` guards the load order. A card module that needs a rule from another logic module breaks this; keep it a leaf.
- There are now two dev areas. `DEV_AREAS` lists both, and `?area=<id>` picks one.

Recorded residuals:

- Moving hazards are outside A1's one-at-a-time rule. A pole may jab while a predator warns or strikes, and two moving hazards may warn or jab at once, because A1's wording covers neither. Root cause: a moving hazard is not a predator, and its fixed cycle is always on show, like the mats'. Bounded: phase 2 keeps each sweep farther than every predator's lungeRange from that predator's loop, keeps moving hazards' danger zones (the sweep grown by touch reach) from overlapping, and the fairness harness steps the real encounter with poles playing. In the dev area the two poles are 200 px apart with danger zones x ± 23 px, so no point is in reach of both, and the nearest predator loop is about 340 px from a pole sweep. Not changed, because holding a pole for a predator would make its cycle unreadable. The pre-PR local review (Codex, 2026-10-24) raised this as a high finding: moving hazards can warn or strike at the same time as other threats. On 2026-10-24 the owner chose to keep ND-8 as written (PRD open question 255). The finding stays for the owner's ND-8 decision on the PR.
- Dev-only cards (ND-19). In the dev area the carp, pole, tadpole and shrimp cards show again on each trip. Root cause: `orderedMetHazards` in `src/logic/session.ts` keeps only `bookFactCards` ids, and `markFactCardFound` refuses unknown ids, so a met id with no book card is dropped. Bounded: a met id is dropped only while no area has its card; once an area has one, it joins the book and is kept. Not changed (no code change) (amended by ADR 0022).
- The carp frames are baked at boot with the other predators' frames, whether or not any area has a carp. Root cause: every predator's frames bake at boot (ADR 0014). Bounded: a few small frames. Bake on demand stays deferred.
- A magnet-pulled tadpole's lane can briefly cross a keep-out. Root cause: the Snack magnet moves the lane start, and the lane moves with it (A19). Bounded: the pull is faster than every snack (ND-28), so the tadpole is eaten soon. Not changed, because the magnet may pull any snack toward the head.
- Per-frame allocation. `MovingHazardView` and `GhostNetView` allocate one small object per moving or drifting spawn per frame through `src/logic` helpers (`movingHazardPhase`, `hazardShapeAt`). Bounded: only areas with those parts pay it. The owner measures frame time when an area adds them (amended by ADR 0022) ([ADR 0013](0013-axolotl-rig-and-frame-budget.md)).
- An area's own tilapia chase (its `tilapia` field) is not checked at run time against the chase limits (ND-3), because it does not pass through `tunedSpecies`. Bounded: phase 2 layout tests must check it; every default is checked.
- The ramp danger bar does not check a numeric "short leash", because ND-12 gives no number. An owner question.
- Fact card claims marked "owner to verify" (C13), in the source comments in `src/logic/factCards.ts`: carp eating axolotl eggs (secondary sources only, no primary study found); "big fish" (unchecked); trajineras "flat-bottomed" (one source only; the UNESCO page could not be read); and the shrimp "tiny" (the source species is about 7 cm), with the shrimp sources describing an Australian species. Never invented: anything unconfirmed stays "owner to verify".
